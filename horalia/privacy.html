Privacy

Horalia — tracks the hours you work for each family and what you're owed.

Horalia collects nothing about you. There is no server, no account to create, no analytics and no ads. What you write down is stored on your iPhone and in your own iCloud account, which neither the app's author nor anyone else can reach.

What the app stores, and where

Horalia stores what you type: the names of the families you work for, their rates, the hours you log, your notes, the expenses you record, and the invoices you close. And, only if you add them, photos of the purchase receipts for those expenses.

That lives in two places, both yours:

  • Your device, in the app's private storage.
  • Your iCloud account, in the private database Apple reserves for each app and each user. That's what keeps your information around if you change phones or lose one, and what makes it show up on your other devices signed in to the same account.

That private database is run by Apple, not by the app's author. The author cannot see it, query it, or receive a copy of anything. If you want to know what Apple does with what you store in iCloud, their privacy policy explains it: apple.com/legal/privacy.

What the app does NOT do

  • It doesn't send your data to any server run by the author. No such server exists.
  • No analytics, no usage measurement, no crash reporting.
  • No ads, and nothing shared with advertisers.
  • No tracking across apps or websites.
  • It never asks for your location, contacts or microphone.
  • It doesn't touch your camera or your photos except when you attach or save a receipt photo (explained in the next section).
  • No third-party code: the only pieces it uses are frameworks from the operating system itself.

Receipt photos

When you record an expense you can attach up to five photos of the purchase receipt, if you want to. It's optional: without photos, the expense is saved just the same.

  • Choosing a photo you already have. This uses the iOS photo picker, which shows your library without handing it to the app: Horalia only receives the photos you pick, nothing else. That's why no permission is needed.
  • Taking the photo right then. Horalia uses the camera only when you tap "Take photo". The first time, iOS asks for your permission.
  • Before saving it, the app shrinks the photo and strips its metadata, including the location where it was taken. So if you share it, it doesn't say where you were.
  • It's stored like everything else: on your device and in your private iCloud. The author can't see it.
  • It isn't included in the PDF invoice. It leaves the app only if you share it or save it to Photos; to save it, iOS asks for permission to add photos, not to see the ones you have.
  • Deleting an expense also deletes its photos. If another of your devices had added photos to it while offline, those may stay in your iCloud; they're deleted along with the app's data from the iCloud settings.

Face ID

You can ask Horalia to lock when it opens. When you do, the app asks the system "is this person the owner of this device?" and receives only a yes or a no. Horalia never sees your face, your fingerprint or any biometric data: that never leaves the device's secure chip and no app can read it.

If the device has no passcode and no biometrics set up, that lock can't protect anything, and the app says so instead of leaving you with a false sense of security.

The PDF receipts

When you close an account, Horalia generates a PDF on your device and offers it for sharing. It goes only where you choose, through the normal iOS share sheet. The app doesn't upload it anywhere.

One thing to keep in mind: that receipt carries the family's name, the days and hours worked, the amounts, and any notes you wrote on each entry. It's a receipt meant to be handed to that family; the note field says so as you type it.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app from your device removes what it kept there. Whatever is in your iCloud is managed from the system settings, in the iCloud section, same as any other app. You don't need to ask anyone: there is no copy somewhere else to request removal from.

Children

Horalia is a work tool for keeping track of hours. It isn't directed at children under 13, and it collects no information from anyone, of any age.
